How much does a small business website cost? (2026 prices)

The honest answer is "anywhere from about $150 a year to many thousands", which isn't much use. So this guide breaks the cost into the pieces you actually pay for, with current prices from each company's own pricing page, and three worked budgets for a restaurant, a clinic and a small shop.

All prices are in US dollars and were checked on 17 November 2026. Companies change prices and run promotions often, so treat them as a guide and check the pricing page before you buy. Sources are listed at the end.

"Running costs" means the domain, hosting or builder plan, and email. Those are the parts every website pays for, so start there.

1. The domain name: about $10–$20 a year

The domain is your address, like yourcafe.com. A .com costs roughly:

Watch the renewal price, not the first-year price. A cheap first year that renews at $23 costs more over five years than a flat $12. Many website builders include a free domain for the first year on yearly plans, then charge their normal rate.

2. Business email: about $7 per person a month

An address like orders@yourcafe.com looks far more trustworthy than a free Gmail address. The two common choices:

  • Google Workspace Business Starter: $7 per user per month on a yearly plan (Gmail, Calendar, Drive). New customers are offered a lower rate for the first year.
  • Microsoft 365 Business Basic: about $7 per user per month after Microsoft's July 2026 price rise (Outlook, Teams, OneDrive).

For one person that's about $84 a year. Some domain registrars and hosts include basic email for less, which is fine if you only need to send and receive mail.

3. Option A: a website builder

Builders host the site for you, handle security updates and include the SSL certificate (the padlock in the browser). You pay monthly and drag and drop your pages. This is the simplest choice for most small businesses.

Two things change the real price more than the headline number:

  • Yearly vs monthly billing. Paying for a year upfront is usually 30–50% cheaper per month.
  • Fees on sales. If you sell online, a 2% or 8% platform fee on top of normal card fees adds up quickly. A shop selling $2,000 a month pays $160 a month in platform fees at 8%, but nothing extra at 0%.

4. Option B: self-hosted WordPress

WordPress itself is free. You rent hosting, install it (most hosts do this in one click), and pick a theme. It gives you the most freedom and the lowest hosting bill, but you are responsible for updates, backups and plugins.

The renewal jump is the big one. Intro hosting prices are often a third to a fifth of the renewal price, and you only get them by paying several years upfront. Budget for the renewal price, and treat the intro discount as a bonus.

5. Option C: paying someone to build it

This is where prices vary the most, because you are paying for time and skill. Marketplace and survey figures:

  • Freelancers: Upwork's guides put a small business website at roughly $1,500–$5,000 and a single landing page at $500–$2,000. Typical web developer rates there are $15–$50 an hour. Fiverr's own guides give a similar $1,000–$5,000 for a basic business site.
  • Agencies: Clutch, which tracks web design agencies and their reviews, says most projects cost under $10,000. US agency rates are typically $100–$149 an hour, while specialists in Wix, Squarespace, WordPress or Shopify charge around $25–$49 an hour.

Whoever builds it, you still pay the running costs from sections 1 and 2, and a hosting or builder plan. Ask before you start:

  • Will the domain and hosting be in my name, on my account?
  • Can I edit the text and photos myself, or do I pay for every change?
  • What is the monthly cost after launch, and what happens if I stop paying it?
  • Do I get all the files if we stop working together?

A good builder answers "yes" to owning the domain, hosting and files. If the domain is registered in someone else's name, you don't fully own your website.

6. Ongoing and optional costs

  • Maintenance plans: commonly $25–$150 a month for a small site (updates, backups, small edits). Builders mostly don't need one; self-hosted WordPress sites benefit from one if you won't do updates yourself.
  • SSL certificate (the padlock): free. Builders and all the hosts above include one. You should not be paying extra for it.
  • Online ordering for restaurants: delivery marketplaces charge 15–30% commission per order. Ordering tools that run on your own website, such as DoorDash Storefront, charge only the card processing fee, which is much cheaper for orders from regular customers.
  • Appointment booking: Calendly has a free plan and paid plans from about $10 a user a month. Square Appointments has a free plan, and paid plans start around $49 per location a month.

Hidden costs checklist

  • Renewal prices. Hosting renews at 3–5× the intro price; some domains jump from $5 to $23.
  • Long prepayments. The lowest hosting prices need 12–48 months paid upfront.
  • Platform fees on sales. 2–10% on some builder plans, on top of card fees.
  • Plan limits. The cheapest plan may not take payments (Wix Light) or may charge higher fees.
  • Add-ons. Booking, ordering, extra email users and paid plugins are often billed per seat, per location or per month.
  • "Free" first-year domains. They renew at the normal price in year two.
  • Your own time. Writing the text and taking good photos is often the slowest part of any website project.

Which option fits you?

  • You want it simple and will edit it yourself: a website builder. Squarespace or Wix for a brochure site, Shopify if selling products is the main point.
  • You're comfortable with a bit of tech and want the lowest yearly cost: self-hosted WordPress, budgeting for the renewal price.
  • You have no time, or need something custom (bookings tied to your system, several languages, a special design): pay a freelancer or agency, and make sure you own the domain, hosting and files.

FAQ

How much should a small business website cost?

With a website builder, about $150–$500 in the first year. A freelancer charges roughly $1,000–$5,000 to build one, plus running costs. Clutch says most agency projects cost under $10,000.

How much does a small business website cost per month?

Builder plans billed yearly run from $4 a month (WordPress.com Personal) to about $29 (Wix Core). Shopify Basic is $19. Add about $7 per person a month for business email.

How much does small business website hosting cost?

Self-hosted WordPress plans start at $1.99–$3.99 a month, but only with a 12–48 month prepay. They renew at $9.99–$17.99 a month, so budget for the renewal price.

What does small business website maintenance cost?

Maintenance plans commonly cost $25–$150 a month for a small site. Builders mostly don't need one. Self-hosted WordPress sites benefit from one if you won't do updates yourself.

What are the hidden costs of a small business website?

Renewal prices, long prepayments, platform fees of 2–10% on sales, and add-ons billed per seat or per month. Hosting often renews at 3–5× the intro price.
