A season in madder

Chalk and black need a third voice. This season it is a deep, earthy red, used almost nowhere.

Every collection starts with a pinboard, and every pinboard eventually gets too loud. This one began with forty swatches and ended with three: an undyed stone linen, a chalk cotton that is not quite white, and a square of madder red that nobody could stop touching.

Madder is the colour of old brick, of a wax seal, of the ink a careful editor uses in a margin. We decided it should behave like that ink: a correction, not a headline. You will find it on this page in the small things — a label, a number, a focus ring — and in no garment at all.

That restraint is the point. A wardrobe built from chalk and shadow can take one strong note. It can take it for years, which is longer than any trend colour stays interesting.

A note on honesty: madder appears here as a colour reference. None of the pieces in this sample collection are madder-dyed, and we make no claim about natural dyeing.

Size guides give flat garment measurements in centimetres, so you can compare them with something you already own.

03

Care that lengthens wear

Each piece carries plain care instructions: wash cooler, dry in shade, press while damp.

04

What we don’t claim

You will not find certifications, factory audits or carbon figures here. This demonstration cannot substantiate them, so it does not make them.
