Training, experience and the way I practise.

Dr. Amina Rahman is a family physician with more than twelve years of practice in hospital and community settings. Her work centres on first-contact care: the visit where a worry is first described, examined and given a plan.

She trained in internal medicine and paediatrics before completing specialist training in family medicine, and now runs a small outpatient practice where each appointment is long enough to ask questions properly.

Before, during and after your visit.

Practical answers about preparing, what happens in the room, and how appointments work.

Not for emergencies

If you have chest pain, difficulty breathing, signs of stroke or heavy bleeding, call your local emergency number or go to the nearest emergency department.

This page offers general information. It is not a substitute for an individual medical assessment.

Before your visit

How should I prepare for my appointment?

Write down the main concern and when it started, list your current medicines, and bring any recent results or letters. If there are several concerns, tell the receptionist when booking so a longer slot can be offered.

Do I need to fast for blood tests?

Only some tests need fasting. If a fasting test is planned, the clinic will tell you in advance and explain for how long. Keep drinking water and do not stop medicines unless you are told to.

Should I bring my medicines?

Yes, ideally in their original boxes, including over-the-counter products, vitamins and herbal remedies. It is the most reliable way to check doses and avoid interactions.

During the consultation

What happens in a first consultation?

You describe the concern, the doctor asks questions and, where needed, examines you. You then talk through what is likely, what is not, and agree a plan, which may include tests, treatment, a review date or a referral.

Can I have a chaperone during an examination?

Yes. You can ask for a chaperone for any examination, and one is always offered for intimate examinations. You can also bring a relative or friend.

When is a video appointment suitable?

Video works for follow-ups, results and medication questions that do not need an examination. New symptoms are usually better seen in person. If video is not appropriate, you will be offered an in-person time instead.

Appointment policies

Is my request a confirmed booking?

No. A request tells the clinic your preferred time. The appointment is only confirmed when the clinic contacts you. Until then, the time may still be offered to someone else.

How do I change or cancel an appointment?

Please let the clinic know at least 24 hours ahead so the time can be offered to another patient. Late arrivals of more than 10 minutes may need to be rebooked.

Can I book for my child?

Yes. A parent or legal guardian should make the request and attend with the child. Older children may be offered some time to talk on their own.

Can a family member speak to the doctor about me?

Only with your permission. Tell the clinic who you are happy for information to be shared with; you can change this at any time.
