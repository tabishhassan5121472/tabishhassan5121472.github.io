Founding story · Spec piece · Plainweave (fictional label)

Writing a voice for a label that wants you to buy less.

The brief
A self-set brief: write the founding story and core voice for a small clothing label that makes fewer, longer-lasting basics. It is modelled on the kind of request small labels often bring.
The audience
People building a practical wardrobe. They are tired of trend copy, suspicious of ‘sustainable’ as a sales word, and usually reading on a phone between other things.
The writing challenge
Say ‘buy less’ on a page whose job is to sell, without the vocabulary every other slow-fashion label already uses: timeless, curated, elevated, effortless.

The approach

  1. Start from use, not material: the shirt you reach for without thinking.
  2. Write the banned-word list before writing a single line of copy.
  3. Keep sentences short enough to read on a bus, and let one longer sentence carry the belief.

The final work

Written for an about page, a packaging insert and a first newsletter.

Start with the piece you reach for without thinking.1 The shirt that moves with you. The layer that makes an ordinary Tuesday2 feel considered.

Plainweave makes fewer things, on purpose. Cotton that softens rather than wears out. Cuts with enough room to sit, carry, cycle and change your mind. Colours that sit quietly beside whatever you already own.

We don’t believe a wardrobe needs more noise. It needs room for the way you actually live, and for the pieces that have earned their place.3

Buy less. Wear it more. Mend it when it asks.4

  2. 2A specific day does more work than ‘everyday’. ↩
  3. 3The one longer sentence. This is the belief. ↩
  4. 4Three imperatives that rise in commitment. The last one is the brand. ↩
Brief a similar piece

Hello, I’m Mira.

I came to writing through editing: other people’s drafts, mostly, and the slow work of finding the sentence they meant to write.

I still think that’s the job. It isn’t decoration. It’s finding the line that makes an idea click, then clearing away everything that stands in front of it.

My work moves between three registers. There are observant essays that take their time, brand voices that have to sound the same on a label and a homepage, and web copy that helps someone take the next step without being pushed.

I work best with people who already know what they’re trying to say and can’t quite get it onto the page. I’m less useful for volume: forty blog posts a month, or copy written for search engines first and people second.

— M.

What should I put in a brief?+

The audience, the format, the purpose and your preferred timing. The checklist above covers the rest. The form here creates a local draft only.

I write for readers, and I structure pages so search engines can follow them. I don’t produce keyword-volume content.

No. It saves a draft in your browser and shows a demo confirmation. Nothing is sent anywhere.
