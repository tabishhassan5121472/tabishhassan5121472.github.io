Clifton

Coastal district · south Karachi

High-rise and mid-rise apartments alongside older bungalows, close to the seafront. Sea air is hard on metal fittings and window frames, so ask how a building is maintained, how water is supplied and what backup power covers.

Ask: maintenance charges, water source, generator capacity.

02

DHA

Planned housing scheme · run by the Defence Housing Authority

A phased, planned scheme with its own rules. Transfers, construction approvals and some utilities go through the authority's own process, so confirm the property's transfer status and any dues early.

Ask: transfer status, outstanding dues, phase-specific rules.

Gulshan-e-Iqbal

Established residential town · east-central Karachi

A large, settled area of houses, portions and apartment blocks, with schools and markets close by. Many houses are split into portions, so check whether a portion has its own meters and an approved plan.

Ask: separate meters, approved plan, parking on the lane.

Buying in Karachi: a practical order of things.

The order matters more than the speed. Most problems we see come from paying a token before the paperwork has been looked at.

  1. Set the whole budget, not just the price.

    Add transfer taxes and fees, legal review, any society or authority charges, and moving and repair costs. Rates change, so ask a tax adviser for current figures.

  2. See the title chain before you fall in love.

    Ask for the sale or lease deed, earlier transfer documents and, for planned schemes, the allotment or file record. Copies are fine at this stage.

  3. Verify with the authority that holds the record.

    Depending on the property, that may be a housing society, DHA or the relevant land or building authority. Ask about an approved plan for any construction.

  4. Put the token agreement in writing.

    If you pay a token (bayana), the agreement should name both parties, the price, the timeline and what happens to the money if either side withdraws.

  5. Transfer, then hand over.

    Pay through traceable banking instruments, complete the transfer at the relevant office, then take keys, meter readings and the latest paid bills on the same day.

Renting: what to settle before you sign.

A good tenancy is mostly a clear one. Settle these in writing and the first year is usually uneventful.

  1. Know what the rent includes.

    Maintenance charges, utilities, generator or water-tanker costs, and parking can be included or extra. Get the answer for this property, not a general one.

  2. Read the agreement for term, notice and increase.

    Check the minimum term, the notice period on both sides, how and when rent can rise, and the deposit amount and conditions for its return.

  3. Register the tenancy.

    In Sindh, tenancies are expected to be registered with the local police under the Sindh Information of Temporary Residence Act, 2015. We'll explain how it's handled for a given property.

  4. Record the condition at move-in.

    Walk the property with the landlord or their representative, list fittings and furniture, take dated photographs, and both sign the inventory.

What do Stated, Estimate and To confirm mean?+

Stated facts come from the listing record. Estimates are our indicative figures and may be wrong. To confirm means we have not yet verified it, and it should be checked before any decision.

Does a viewing request book an appointment?+

No. A request tells the agent which home and which times suit you. An appointment only exists once the agent confirms a time with the owner and with you.
